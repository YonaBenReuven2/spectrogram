Settings

The browser's echo cancelling, noise suppression and automatic gain are turned off: they change the overtones. If Windows adds its own (Sound, your mic, Audio enhancements), turn those off too.

Teach it your voice

The register meter starts on a generic scale. How strong the overtones come out also depends on your voice, the vowel and the mic, so for a reading that fits you, hold one comfortable note each way for a couple of seconds, on the same vowel (“ah” works well).

Reading it

Chest voice has a long closed phase in each vibration of the folds, which puts a lot of energy in the overtones: the 2nd and 3rd harmonic are often louder than the fundamental, and many more follow. In head voice the folds barely close, the fundamental carries the sound and the overtones fall away fast. Mix sits between. The meter combines three things: all the overtones together against the fundamental, the fundamental against the 2nd harmonic (H1−H2), and how fast the harmonics fall away (tilt).

Vowels move things too: a harmonic that lands on a resonance of the mouth gets a lift whatever the register. Compare like with like: the same vowel, a similar pitch.

The cents under each overtone are where it sits against the piano's tuning. They are the same for every voice (the 3rd harmonic is always a fifth 2 cents wide, the 5th a third 14 cents narrow), shifted by however sharp or flat you are.
